From Kansas to Vectors: An Illustrated Guide

Understanding AI (Artificial Intelligence) Summarization, Subword Tokens (Byte-Pair Encoding / BPE), Vector Databases (Qdrant HNSW Graphs), and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) through a single passage from The Wonderful Wizard of Oz.

The Source Text • Chapter 1: The Cyclone

"Dorothy lived in the midst of the great Kansas prairies, with Uncle Henry, who was a farmer, and Aunt Em, who was the farmer's wife. Their house was small, for the lumber to build it had to be carried by wagon many miles. There were four walls, a floor and a roof, which made one room; and this room contained a rusty looking cookstove, a cupboard for the dishes, a table, three or four chairs, and the beds. Uncle Henry and Aunt Em had a big bed in one corner, and Dorothy a little bed in another corner. There was no garret at all, and no cellar—except a small hole dug in the ground, called a cyclone cellar, where the family could go in case one of those great whirlwinds arose, mighty enough to crush any building in its path."
— L. Frank Baum, The Wonderful Wizard of Oz (1900)

1. What Are Tokens? (Text into Lego Bricks & Numbers)

Plain English (For Everyone)

A computer processor is an electronic calculator. It has no eyes to see letters and no brain to comprehend words. Before an AI can read about Dorothy and her cyclone cellar, it must snap the sentence apart into standardized chunks called tokens.

Think of tokens like LEGO bricks: Common words like "the" or "house" get their own single brick. Rare words or proper names like "Dorothy" get split into two smaller bricks: ["Dor", "othy"]. Every single unique brick in the computer's dictionary has an assigned serial number (Token ID). The computer turns Baum's storytelling into a sequence of numbers.

The Mathematics • Byte-Pair Encoding (Sennrich et al., 2016)

The vocabulary $V$ is built iteratively. Starting from the alphabet base $\Sigma$, the most frequent pair of adjacent tokens is merged:

$$\text{freq}(c_i, c_j) = \max_{(a, b) \in V_k \times V_k} \sum_{w \in C} \text{count}(a \circ b \in w), \quad V_{k+1} = V_k \cup \{ c_i \circ c_j \}$$

To retain the order of words in Kansas, sinusoidal positional encodings $PE$ (Vaswani et al., 2017) are added to the token vectors:

$$PE_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right), \quad PE_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d}}\right)$$

2. Embeddings & Vector Space (Words as Coordinates in Thought-Space)

Plain English (For Everyone)

How does the AI know that a cyclone and a whirlwind are almost the same thing, even though they share no common letters?

Imagine a giant map of human knowledge. On this map, words that mean similar things live next door to each other, while unrelated things are continents apart. Unlike a paper map with only 2 directions (North/South, East/West), an AI thought-map has 384 to 1536 dimensions. When Dorothy's paragraph is sent through an embedding model, it is turned into a single point: an address of 384 numbers.

The Mathematics • Cosine Similarity & Angular Distance

The semantic relevance between chunk $\mathbf{u}$ and query $\mathbf{v}$ is the cosine of the angle between them (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019):

$$\text{CosineSimilarity}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\|_2 \|\mathbf{v}\|_2} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^d u_i v_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d u_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d v_i^2}}$$

When vectors are pre-normalized during ingestion ($\|\mathbf{u}\| = \|\mathbf{v}\| = 1$), this reduces to a blazing-fast dot product: $\sum_{i=1}^d u_i v_i$.

3. How Qdrant Works (Finding Needles with Hierarchical Navigable Small World / HNSW Graphs)

Plain English (For Everyone)

Imagine a library containing 10 million books. If someone asks for a book about underground storm shelters in Kansas, checking every book one by one would take weeks.

Instead, Qdrant builds an Express Highway Network (technically called an HNSW Graph):

  • Top Layer (The Interstate Highway): Connects a few major concept cities ("Science", "History", "Farming & Agriculture"). The search zooms straight to "Farming & Agriculture".
  • Middle Layer (The State Highway): Branches out to "Livestock", "Farm Machinery", and "Prairie Weather Hazards". The search hops to "Prairie Weather Hazards".
  • Bottom Layer (The Local Driveway): Houses all individual paragraphs. The search lands on Dorothy's cyclone cellar chunk in under 3 milliseconds.
The Mathematics • HNSW Skip-List Probability (Malkov & Yashunin, 2018)

The layer $l$ of an inserted vector is assigned using an exponential decay distribution parameterized by $m_L = 1 / \ln(M)$:

$$l = \left\lfloor -\ln(\text{uniform}(0, 1)) \cdot m_L \right\rfloor, \quad \text{guaranteeing } |V_l| \approx \frac{|V_{l-1}|}{M}$$

Time Complexity: Drops from linear brute-force $\mathcal{O}(N \cdot d)$ down to logarithmic $\mathcal{O}(\log(N) \cdot d)$. Searching 10 million passages takes ~24 graph hops instead of 10,000,000 checks.

4. How Summarization Works (Attention, Distillation & Synthesis via Large Language Models / LLMs)

Plain English (For Everyone)

When you summarize Dorothy's paragraph, you naturally ignore the rusty cookstove and the chairs, because your brain focuses on the vital plot facts: Dorothy lives with her aunt and uncle in a tiny one-room Kansas house, equipped with a storm cellar to protect against crushing cyclones.

An AI model does this with a mathematical highlighter called Self-Attention: every word casts an attention spotlight onto other words in the sentence. The word "cyclone" casts intense spotlights onto "cellar", "Kansas", and "crush".

The Mathematics • Scaled Dot-Product Attention (Vaswani et al., 2017)

The attention calculation queries $Q$ against keys $K$ to weight values $V$:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V$$

5. The Full Lifecycle in Action: Grounded Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

When a researcher types into the chat drawer: "Where does Dorothy's family hide during a violent tornado?", here is what happens:

Step 1: Embedding & Qdrant Fast Retrieval

The question is vectorized and hops through Qdrant's HNSW graph in 2.8 ms, returning Dorothy's Chapter 1 chunk with a 91.8% semantic match.

Step 2: Grounded Prompt Injection

The retrieved chunk is injected into the prompt context: "Answer strictly using the citation context below..."

Step 3: Verified Answer with Interactive Citation

The AI responds: "Dorothy's family hides in a small hole dug in the ground called a 'cyclone cellar' [Citation 1], designed to protect them from whirlwinds mighty enough to crush any building."

The Big Picture: Tokens turn words into numbers • Vectors turn numbers into meanings • Qdrant organizes meanings into express highways • Attention distills meanings into summaries and answers.
